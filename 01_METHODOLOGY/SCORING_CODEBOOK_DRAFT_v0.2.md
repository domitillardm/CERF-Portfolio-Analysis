## 4. Evidence requirements and inclusion/exclusion by code

All five labels and tests are **[SOURCE]**. The operational guidance and illustrative contrasts below are **[PROVISIONAL]**, designed to apply those tests without narrowing or extending them silently. No actual monitoring record is newly scored.

### Code 1 — Activity described

**Source test:** Does the text only explain what CERF did?

**Provisional evidence requirement:** an interpretable description of CERF's action. Concise text may suffice; do not demand a deliverable or an outcome, an attachment, an exact date or independent corroboration merely to establish what the narrative says CERF did.

**Include provisionally:** a described visit, meeting, exchange or exploratory engagement when the text supports action but not any higher achieved level. Missing output/outcome fields alone do not justify 6.

**Exclude from this level when a higher source test is supported:** a distinct delivered product/service; another actor's use or documented next step; or reported external change in any listed domain. Do not settle on 1 merely because an activity description appears first in the row.

**Ambiguities:** whether a terse title reports action or just names a proposed activity; action and plan mixed together; whether a workshop is only an activity description or a distinct delivered service. A dated entry is not proof of occurrence. A description entirely in future tense requires the plan-only review below rather than an invented achieved action.

**Monitoring-derived illustration:** a fragment describes a visit and meeting, with no further result detail. It illustrates the action/information distinction; no code is allocated to its source row. A hypothetical unexplained project identifier lacks the same interpretable content.

### Code 2 — Output delivered

**Source test:** Does the text confirm a distinct product, service or completed deliverable?

**Provisional evidence requirement:** identify what distinct product/service/deliverable the text confirms as delivered. Completion may be reported in monitoring; the source does not require possession or inspection of the underlying document. A service is an output, not only a physical report. A formally commissioned contract or quantified participant count is not universally required.

**Include provisionally:** a completed analytical report or white paper, delivered training/technical service, produced recommendations or delivered tool, if the wording confirms the relevant output. A named complete interim product can qualify without assuming the entire assignment is complete.

**Exclude:** only intentions to commission/produce; generic expenditure or recruitment without a distinct output; a product title with no delivery confirmation. Do not confuse intended usefulness or availability with actual use by another actor. If a higher achieved source test is met, retain the higher level.

**Ambiguities:** a draft may be a delivered draft product, while a document still being drafted is not a completed final product; report the actual status. A meeting may deliver a distinct facilitation/training service, but mere attendance is not automatically such a service. These are interpretations requiring review, not “commissioned-service” gates from I1.

**Monitoring-derived illustration:** the monitoring describes a resulting white paper and says it can inform future discussion. The delivered product and expected future use must be read separately. This does not classify the complete source activity.

### Code 3 — Concrete use or follow-up reported

**Source test:** Did another actor use the output or take a documented next step?

**Provisional evidence requirement:** identify another actor or its anonymised role, the use or actual next step described, and its relation to the focal activity/output. The wording must support an achieved use/step rather than an intention to do so. “Documented” can include a sufficiently clear monitoring account; I1 does not require an independently inspected attachment for every next step.

**Include provisionally:** another actor uses the analysis in a specified discussion or roadmap; requests/commissions a further action linked to the work; or takes a concrete follow-up step. Use and next step are alternative routes under the source's “or”; output use is not mandatory if a documented next step is supported.

**Exclude:** only CERF staff continuing their own work with no identified other actor; dissemination alone as automatic use; expressed future interest without an achieved step; same-track chronology as automatic linkage. If the same evidence also describes an achieved external change in a listed domain, examine 4 rather than capping it at 3.

**Ambiguities:** another actor can potentially be a recipient, partner, ministry, company, embassy unit or implementing organisation depending on its role relative to CERF and the focal provider. The excerpt does not define “another actor” as necessarily fully independent or outside every implementation arrangement. Broad permission for all internal use is withdrawn; role boundaries remain for review. A signed partnership may describe both a next step and changed cooperation; legal status alone does not settle the level.

**Monitoring-derived illustration:** an analytical assignment's narrative mentions a later discussion using the analysis. The question is who used it and what happened, not whether the study was merely meant to inform dialogue. Another fragment refers to roadmap inclusion alongside stalled investment; reported use and unrealised investment must be separated.

### Code 4 — Change in an external actor reported

**Source test:** Does the text describe a change in policy, behaviour, practice, capacity, cooperation, finance or market activity? The classification label specifies an **external actor**.

**Provisional evidence requirement:** a text-supported account of what changed, in whom/which external actor, in at least one of the seven domains. A comparison over time, measured baseline, formal instrument or realised operational consequence can clarify the claim, but I1 makes none a universal prerequisite. A sufficiently specific monitoring account can report a change even when it is uncorroborated; its evidence limitations remain explicit.

**Include provisionally:** described changes in policy; behaviour or routine practice; capacity (including a described change in ability, with the learning boundary still open); cooperation or relationships between actors; financing/resource arrangements; or market activity. A change in capacity or cooperation is not required also to produce formal policy reform, institutional restructuring, investment disbursement or market sales. Specific changes can be modest; I1 states no minimum magnitude.

**Exclude:** mere descriptions of what CERF did, output delivery, expectations of change, or next steps that do not describe external change. Do not infer learning automatically from training attendance, changed cooperation automatically from meeting attendance, or realised finance from prospects. Vague slogans can fail to support the change claimed, but this is an information judgement, not a blanket exclusion of soft-result domains.

**Ambiguities:** how much detail makes “capacity strengthened” or “cooperation improved” a description of change rather than a vague assertion; how reported learning relates to capacity; when a cooperation next step becomes changed cooperation; whose status counts as external; how to treat adverse changes. A non-binding MoU can describe changed cooperation, while a binding instrument can still be only planned. There is no automatic legal-bindingness rule. Formal adoption can report policy change without proving implementation; implementation can be reported without proving wider impact.

**Illustrations, not record scores:** available monitoring mentions signings, changed cooperation, framework establishment and broad governance/capacity claims. Those fragments motivate questions about the actor and actual reported change, rather than a predetermined 3/4 answer. **Hypothetical contrast:** a narrative specifically says partners moved from occasional contact to a functioning joint coordination arrangement. This illustrates the cooperation domain even without a new law. A narrative merely says the workshop “will strengthen cooperation” expresses potential instead.

### Code 6 — Insufficient information

**Source test:** Is the information too vague to support codes 1–4?

**Provisional evidence requirement:** explain which action/result cannot be established from the available text and identify missing or ambiguous information. Do not require a separate “eligible implemented record” finding as a condition stated in I1. Equally, do not use 6 as a catch-all for every status or scope problem.

**Include provisionally:** information so vague that no interpretable CERF action, confirmed distinct delivery, another actor's use/step or described external change can be supported. An unexplained identifier or generic theme may be insufficient even if the text is lengthy. Conflicting/inaccessible records can create an information problem, but their precise treatment is an extension for review.

**Exclude:** insufficient evidence for 4 when 1, 2 or 3 is supported; no independent corroboration alone; a blank title if substantive information is elsewhere; absence of outcome reporting when an output is clearly confirmed. A detailed plan without achievement is not automatically “vague”: the source explicitly permits future Yes in place of achieved result but does not specify the achieved-code treatment for that edge case.

**Ambiguities:** sparse description versus uninterpretable content; an unavailable linked source with a clear monitoring account; irreconcilable narratives; planned-only or cancelled records. Keep unresolved cases visible for human decision rather than changing inclusion or filling codes now.

**Illustration:** the monitoring includes missing titles with meaningful descriptions/implementation elsewhere. Title absence alone cannot establish insufficient information. A hypothetical row containing only an unexplained reference illustrates the vagueness question, not a new classification of a source record.

## 5. Core boundaries after reconciliation

| Boundary | Source-grounded distinction | Provisional clarification / review question |

| 1 versus 6 | Text explains what CERF did versus text too vague to support any of 1–4. | Do not equate no higher-result reporting with insufficient information. Clarify future-only and terse-title cases; do not impose independent verification as a minimum. |
| 1 versus 2 | Activity description versus confirmed distinct product, service or completed deliverable. | Define when workshops/meetings deliver a distinct service; do not require a commissioned contract or paper artefact. |
| 2 versus 3 | Confirmed delivery versus another actor's use or documented next step. | Identify the other actor and achieved action; don't mistake intended usefulness/dissemination for realised use. |
| 3 versus 4 | Another actor's use/step versus a reported external change in one of seven domains. | The domains overlap: a next step can also describe changed cooperation/capacity. | Result versus evidence strength | Highest achieved level supported by the text is a classification of reported achievement. | More independent evidence can strengthen support without changing semantic level. Weak support must be visible; an existing rationale or repeated report is not corroboration. |
| Result versus future potential | Potential is separate Yes/No and is not a higher level. | Yes can accompany any achieved code, including an information-poor record if a future intention is nevertheless clear. No future intention is not proof that no future effect is possible. |

Use 6 only when the information is too vague for 1–4; do not use numerical MAX over 1,2,3,4,6. Separately apply the future-potential rule. ## 6. Future potential — binary source classification

**[SOURCE]** Record yes/no separately. Use yes when the text describes an intention, plan or expected effect alongside, or instead of, an achieved result. Potential never raises the achieved level.

| Future field | Source or interpretation | Operational explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Yes | **[SOURCE]** Intention, plan or expected effect is described. | Applies whether or not an achieved result is also present. The source does not require proof of plausibility, high probability, a named future investor or an independently verified commitment. Such detail can be noted separately. |
| No | Binary category required by **[SOURCE]**; its default is not expressly elaborated. | **[PROVISIONAL]** For sufficiently readable text with no described intention/plan/expected effect, record No meaning “not described in the text”. An explicit statement that nothing further will happen is not required. |
| Review status / uncertainty | **[PROVISIONAL]**, separate field, not a third potential category. | Missing/unreadable text, contradictory plans or ambiguous tense may require Pending/Not assessed plus a note. Do not invent Unclear as a source-authorised classification or silently replace existing blanks. |

**Important unresolved edge case:** a detailed plan-only record has future Yes under I1. The excerpt does not explicitly select 1, 6, blank/not assessed or a separate status for its achieved-result field. Do not infer either achieved delivery or automatic insufficiency from that instruction. This is R1 for review; no score or automatic exclusion is applied.

Potential can remain conditional or stalled; distinguish a plan that is still described from an abandoned plan and record the reporting date. Whether a clearly cancelled intention should remain Yes as historically stated or No as current status is R6. The excerpt does not settle the timing convention. Track-level potential synthesis remains an extension.

## 7. Evidence basis, strength, confidence and review

**[PROJECT CONSTRAINT]** Distinguish reported results from independently verified outcomes. The screenshot does not establish an evidence-strength hierarchy or require external corroboration as a result-code gate.

**[PROVISIONAL — retained extension]:** preserve the existing evidence-basis labels: Monitoring claim only; Named or linked source; Independently corroborated; Unclear or missing. These describe provenance, not a reliable numerical ladder. A named source is not an inspected source, and inspecting the delivered report does not verify a different claim that a recipient used it or changed behaviour. Record source access, inspection date/reviewer, independence and which exact claim is supported. Repeated monitoring is not independent corroboration.

**[PROVISIONAL — not in I1]:** a separate strength field could retain v0.1's Strong/Moderate/Weak/Unassessable proposals: Strong for specifically supported claims with meaningful independent/authoritative direct support and addressed contradictions; Moderate for inspected direct documentation with limitations; Weak for interpretable monitoring claims/uninspected references; Unassessable for inadequate support detail. Human agreement is needed on these categories and definitions. They do not overwrite existing labels or change result codes automatically. Confidence/interpretive ambiguity, actual independent verification and human approval are separate facts; no numeric reliability weights or thresholds are introduced.

A capacity/cooperation change reported in monitoring can meet the source semantic test without being independently verified. State that limitation. Neither a reported code 4 nor strong supporting evidence alone proves CERF causality, wider impact or programme success.

## 8. Applying the source tests across A, B and C

I1 does not distinguish A/B/C or provide country-track aggregation rules. The following are **[PROVISIONAL PROJECT EXTENSIONS]**, preserving the established purpose of each approach rather than attributing them to the excerpt.

| Approach | Evidence scope | Consistent application and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — standalone activity | The focal activity's defined monitoring record. | Apply I1's highest achieved text-supported test and separate binary potential. Do not use another row or old scores/rationales as supporting evidence. Underlying-document access and cut-off must be specified consistently. |
| B — contextualised activity | Focal activity plus full chronological country-track history, including subsequent records within an agreed evidence cut-off. | Apply the same tests, including another actor at 3 and all seven change domains at 4. Additional context must describe something relevant to the focal activity/output; it cannot automatically transfer another activity's achievement. Explicit linkage and contradiction handling are provisional safeguards. Preserve A independently; B need not be greater than A. |
| C — holistic country-track | Agreed combined evidence for a reviewed provisional/confirmed country-track. | Once this unit/scope is approved, use the same highest achieved source tests on the combined text. Record supporting activities, extent/coverage, gaps and contradictory claims. Do not silently require structural reform, widespread change, multiple success cases or a fully evidenced causal chain beyond the source tests. A single supported achievement does not imply every activity achieved it. |

A track narrative can explain a possible pathway without establishing causality. A coherent pathway is useful contextual analysis, not a newly imposed necessary threshold that erases a supported result. The precise C synthesis protocol, treatment of isolated/contradictory events and unresolved memberships remain R7. C is not implemented by copying a pre-existing maximum or aggregating old scores.

D1/D2 remain proposed separate summaries (maxima over eligible standalone/contextualised results). The source's highest-achieved instruction supports the semantic ordering of 1–4; it does not approve dataset aggregation, eligibility/review requirements, tie handling or CERF-M001. Code 6 must not outrank 4 in later maxima; no eligible result must remain explicit rather than becoming a fake numerical value. No maximum is calculated here.

## 9. Illustrations and treatment of earlier example annex

All examples here are **[PROVISIONAL]** teaching contrasts, not activity/track scores. Actual monitoring fragments and exact source pointers remain in the confidential v0.2 annex outside Git. The v0.1 annex remains historical; its suggestions that legal bindingness or institutional transition distinguish all cooperation outcomes must not be reused as current rules.

- A described visit differs from an uninterpretable project reference; missing outcome fields alone do not decide between 1 and 6.
- A white paper confirmed as delivered differs from a paper that “can inform” future discussion: delivery and expected future use occupy separate fields.
- A specific recipient using findings in a roadmap differs from CERF staff's own plans to disseminate the report; the another-actor condition must be examined.

- An intention or expected effect without achievement still triggers future Yes. Its achieved-result classification is unresolved in the excerpt, so these records must be flagged before scoring.
