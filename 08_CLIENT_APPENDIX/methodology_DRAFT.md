# DRAFT — CERF portfolio classification methodology

**Provisional; not approved for client circulation.** Updated 28 October 2026 from surviving project artifacts. No formal human validation or independent outcome verification is implied. Historical generated scaffolds remain retained; this is the populated current draft.

## Data and analytical unit

The owner's anonymised monitoring workbook contains 249 activity records. Preparation/anonymisation were owner-reported; this analysis does not independently certify those upstream processes. Dates, evidence fields, original classifications, identifiers and decision history are preserved in an immutable detailed workbook. Activities are organised chronologically within provisional country-tracks, retaining uncertain dates and unresolved membership.

The consolidated analysis contains 93 defined provisional country-track units;14 activities remain in 7 explicitly labelled unresolved holding groups. These are not additional confirmed tracks. Nine same-country case/whitespace aliases were consolidated with lineage, and two source-explicit programme placements were recorded provisionally. This is not a verified programme census or a count of independent causal interventions.

## Classification framework

The visible Inception Report Appendix B table directs use of the highest achieved result supported by text:1 activity described;2 output delivered;3 another actor's concrete use/follow-up;4 external policy, behaviour, practice, capacity, cooperation, finance or market change reported;6 information insufficient for 1–4. The complete report/version/citation is not available here. Operational extensions are labelled provisional.

Future potential is separate Yes/No, not a higher achievement level. Evidence strength and classification confidence remain separate from result code. Planned and completed stages are distinguished. Unresolved, settled no-achieved and cancelled statuses are not automatically recoded as6. Reported external learning/cooperation can support4 without a new requirement for enacted law or independent verification. Monitoring claims are not independently verified outcomes.

## Five outputs

A classifies focal activity monitoring. B considers the full chronological track, requiring focal relevance rather than automatically transferring other activities' results. C interprets combined country-track monitoring. D1 and D2 are maxima over eligible A and B respectively. Code 6 is excluded from ordinal maxima; all-6 gives insufficient-information status. Missing eligible classifications retain an incomplete maximum and known lower bound; cancelled eligibility/exclusions are preserved. These are provisional AI-based summaries, not highest-human-checked scores.

## Implementation independence — material limitation

The retrospective code audit establishes that original B started from A with explicit contextual changes; original singleton C copied A. Multi-activity C had separate authored narrative/code entries, and no universal C=max formula was found. C assignment precedes maxima in code; pilot C was separately locked before D, but full-run C/D were frozen together and separate blinded authoring cannot be demonstrated. Earlier pilot exposure is acknowledged. Therefore these outputs must not be presented as independently assessed methods.

All available ordinal country-track pairs agree, but53 of 82 all-three-comparable units are singleton, where identical evidence naturally limits contrast. Shared highest-level definitions also favour agreement in multi-activity units. The figures describe recorded-output consistency, not accuracy, method equivalence or independent validation. D1/D2 are intentionally deterministic, not separate judgements.

Independent engine/SQL checks validate file/calculation behaviour, not the truth of reported outcomes or judgement independence. ## Coverage and interpretation

Usable numerical outputs include code 6:235 A and 224 B of 249 activities;88 C,83 D1 and 84 D2 of 93 tracks. Valid ordinal1–4 denominators are233,222,87,82 and 83 respectively. Remaining nonnumeric statuses are preserved. Fourteen mappings and 3 factual-stage gaps remain unresolved; two later diagnostic questions are separate and do not automatically invalidate scores.

Country summaries count tracks once and display valid denominators and status coverage. Means of ordinal1–4 codes are exploratory descriptions, not precise effectiveness measures, success rates or causal estimates. Different missingness can change means without paired score changes. Independent case-study comparison requires confirmed scale/missing-code equivalence and exact unit matching; it has not been conducted. A separately blinded non-singleton assessment would be needed before claiming independent method agreement. Human methodological/factual/confidentiality review and explicit release remain required.

Agreement with original C is30/33 (90.91%); against D1 it is25/28 (89.29%) and against D2,26/29 (89.66%). On the same28-track subset all three rates are25/28. Three numerical disagreements are lower in the fresh assessment, with one two-level and two one-level gaps; the unresolved case is not a numerical decrease.

These findings show substantial endpoint stability and specific interpretive sensitivities, not independent outcome verification, a confirmed error rate or proof of systematic generosity/conservatism. Freeze-before-reveal and lack of prior access are recorded by the assessor/user and supported by masked-input matching, but no externally trusted access/timestamp record is supplied. Neither assessment is automatically preferred. Four focused human adjudications are recommended before adopting final ratings. No authoritative/frozen scores or methodology changed; no broad rescoring is initiated. This appendix remains subject to human review and is not approved for client release.
